Residential

First-time buyers

Buying your first home involves many steps. We explain how much you may be able to borrow, help you get an agreement in principle so sellers take your offer seriously, and look at schemes and lenders that suit first-time buyers.

  • Budgeting and deposit planning, including gifted deposits
  • Agreement in principle before you make offers
  • Help dealing with estate agents and solicitors through to completion
Residential

Moving home

We check whether you can move ("port") your existing mortgage, compare it with a new deal, and keep your sale and purchase in step.

  • Porting compared with a new lender
  • Liaison with agents on both your sale and your purchase
  • Advice on any early repayment charges
Early repayment chargesIf you leave your current mortgage early, your lender may charge an early repayment charge.
Residential

Remortgaging

When your fixed or tracker deal ends, your lender will usually move you to its standard variable rate. We review your options with your current lender and across the market, ideally three to six months before your deal ends.

  • Product transfers and remortgages compared
  • Raising funds for home improvements
  • Changing the term or repayment type
Remortgaging may involve costsYou may have to pay an early repayment charge to your existing lender if you remortgage, as well as fees to the new lender.
Think carefully before securing other debts against your home. Adding debts to your mortgage may mean you pay more interest overall, because you repay them over a longer term.
Investment

Buy-to-let

We arrange buy-to-let finance for new landlords and experienced portfolio landlords, whether you buy personally or through a limited company, on residential or commercial property.

  • First-time and portfolio landlords
  • Limited company and SPV purchases
  • HMOs, multi-unit blocks and holiday lets
  • Let-to-buy and consumer buy-to-let
RegulationMost buy-to-let mortgages are not regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. Consumer buy-to-let mortgages, for example where you let a property you inherited or used to live in, are regulated under separate rules.
Residential

Contractors & self-employed

Some lenders struggle with income that doesn't come from a standard payslip. We work with lenders who can assess contractors on their day rate, and with lenders who accept part-time, zero-hours and self-employed income.

  • Day-rate contractors, including those working through umbrella companies or their own limited company
  • Sole traders, partners and company directors
  • Recently self-employed applicants
Residential

Interest-only mortgages

With an interest-only mortgage your monthly payments cover only the interest. The amount you borrowed stays the same and must be repaid in full at the end of the term. Lenders will need a credible plan for repaying it, such as the sale of another property, investments or pension funds.

With an interest-only mortgage you will still owe the full amount borrowed at the end of the term. If your repayment plan falls short, you may have to sell your home.
Specialist

Expat & international buyers

If you live abroad, earn in a foreign currency or are new to the UK, fewer lenders will consider you. We have access to specialist and alternative lenders who regularly lend to expats and international buyers.

Currency riskIf your income is in a different currency from your mortgage, changes in the exchange rate may increase your repayments and the amount you owe in sterling.
Short-term

Bridging finance

Bridging loans are short-term secured loans, for example when a chain breaks and you need to complete on a purchase before your sale goes through, when you buy at auction, or when a property needs work before it can be mortgaged.

Higher cost and riskBridging loans usually cost more than standard mortgages and need a clear exit plan, such as a sale or refinance. Bridging loans secured on your own home are regulated by the FCA. Those for investment or business purposes may not be.
Commercial

Specialist & commercial finance

We arrange funding for projects that fall outside standard lending criteria.

  • Commercial premises and owner-occupied business property
  • Conversions, refurbishments and rebuilds
  • Land and development finance
  • Mixed-use and semi-commercial property
RegulationCommercial mortgages and finance for business purposes are not generally regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. The Financial Ombudsman Service and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may not cover them.
